# Procedural shape decode status

The cooked 2026-10-19 procedural assets were recovered from their IoStore
blocks and decompressed. Their serialized templates contain catalog variants,
default dimensions, labels, categories, and asset identifiers. They do not
contain collision vertices or triangle meshes.

This is demonstrated by assets with different visible geometry sharing the
same serialized template bytes:

- `PB_DefaultMicroBrick`, `PB_DefaultMicroRamp`, and several micro-wedge assets
  share the same five `[1,1,1]` through `[5,5,5]` variants.
- normal, inverted, outer, and inner ramp-corner assets share the same twelve
  dimension variants.
- the geometry changes while the serialized template does not.

The remaining geometry is therefore generated by Brickadia's native code from
the asset identity. It cannot be recovered as a mesh from these `.uasset`
payloads. `build-procedural-shape-probe.cjs` provides isolated in-game point
samples for empirically identifying the solid portions without inventing
collision geometry.

The returned 225-case probe placed 358 of 676 bricks and blocked 318. After
removing the 226 labels/instruction bricks, this resolves to 159 colliding
shape/probe pairs and 66 surviving pairs, with no partial cases. The normalized
results are stored in `procedural-shape-occupancy.json` in the manifest's sample
order. These measurements also establish that the normal ramp's full-height
side is local `-X`; the convex ramp model follows that orientation.

The measured patterns support exact convex triangular-prism models for normal
and inverted ramps, default wedges, micro-ramps, side wedges, side-wedge tiles,
and micro-wedges. Complex corners, crests, arches, and round shapes retain their
measured occupancy but remain conservative envelopes pending denser sampling.

Four subsequent 5x5x5 probes measured 2,500 isolated cases: 1,772 solid, 728
empty, and no partial failures. The complete normalized grids are stored in
`dense-procedural-shape-occupancy.json`. The grids show that the complex corner
and crest surfaces do not reduce to the simple planes suggested by nine broad
samples. They remain conservative envelopes; the measured grids are retained
as evidence rather than promoted to approximate models that would introduce
false collision failures.
